Stride-averaged traces

Averaged over 11 left and 11 right stride cycles, each stretched to its own cycle length. The shaded band is the stride-to-stride spread of this run — how much this runner varied. It is a different quantity from the measurement error above, and the two are never added. Cadence 161 steps per minute.

The stride count may be wrong by a cycle or two. Stride segmentation is the one part of this pipeline with no prior measurement behind it, and its own validation bar — +/-1 stride on 3 clips against mocap — came out 2 of 3, so it is recorded as failed. The traces above are therefore a convenience: the error figures in the reading do not depend on them, and per-frame traces are what a default run shows.

Trunk leanexperimental

Read this as a direction, not a number. Trunk lean carries a measured −2.34° bias on side-on lab footage, is +1.92° [+0.583, +3.204] worse across labs than on the tune set, and is the worst-tracked quantity in the pipeline at any frame rate: r = +0.764, against +0.99 for the knee and thigh. The lifter also does not place its own upper-trunk point consistently in two different labs, and trunk lean is that axis.

What this does and does not measure

It measures

Two sagittal angles per leg, from one side-on video: knee flexion, and thigh inclination against gravity. Both are reported with the error this pipeline carries on footage from a lab it had never seen. Left and right are always shown separately and never averaged.

It does not measure

Anything about health. This is a measurement of joint angles and nothing else: there is no score, no flag, no colour and no comparison against other people anywhere in this document. It is not a clinical assessment and makes no health claim of any kind.

It also does not measure which leg was nearer the camera. You declared that with --near-side, because the pipeline inferred it correctly on only 9 of 11 calibrated clips with nothing to flag the failures. If the side was declared wrongly, the two error columns above are swapped.

What would make the next clip better

Film with the rear camera, from the side, with the runner crossing the whole frame at a steady pace, at 50 fps or faster, in good light. Everything the checks above warned about is a condition the error was not measured in.

The error is largely a per-subject offset -- one sealed subject carried a +0.56° knee bias and another +12.11° -- but it is NOT a removable one: within-subject SD is 4.5-9.7°, and even an oracle per-subject correction would cut MAE by only 17% (knee) and 11% (thigh), leaving 4.8-7.0°.

The SHAPE is tracked well -- knee r +0.77 to +0.92 -- so the trace's form is more trustworthy than its absolute level.

Why the filming instructions ask for 60 fps

These are frame-rate penalties measured in-domain on the tune set, against a native 100 Hz reference — not on new footage. They are the reason a faster clip is worth filming: 67% of the knee’s 30 fps penalty is incurred between 50 and 30 fps. They are not a correction you can apply to the error above, and this report never combines them with it.
