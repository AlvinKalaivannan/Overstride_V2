# Overstride — review brief

**For:** project owner and one reviewer with no prior exposure to this work.
**Covers:** state as of commit `aec0cf6`, the eight commits that produced it,
every published claim that moved, and five decisions that need a call.

This is not a status report. §7 says where the work is weakest, and §8 asks for
decisions. Both are the point.

---

## 1. The project, for a cold reader

**The question.** Injury screening for runners is normally done in a motion
capture lab. If the same lower-limb kinematics were recovered from a single
camera instead, how much classification performance is lost? The deliverable was
always that degradation curve, measured — and a negative result was declared
valid in advance.

**The data.** The Ferber / Running Injury Clinic dataset: 1,798 runners, 2,506
treadmill sessions, marker-based mocap. Labels describe injury status *at the
time of testing*, so nothing here forecasts future injury. Joint-angle waveforms
are not stored — they are regenerated by the archive's own MATLAB pipeline.

**Screening failed, and the reason matters.** Zero of 60 pre-registered tests
survived multiple-comparison correction. Worse, a model built only from
*questionnaire missingness and collection year* — no physiology whatsoever —
scores 0.775 pooled and 0.863 on patellofemoral pain. Questionnaire completeness
tracks the study wave, and the waves differ in injury mix. **Most of what looks
like a gait signal in this dataset is paperwork.**

**The pivot.** Comparing a runner's injured limb to *their own* healthy limb, in
the same trial, eliminates that confound by construction: demographics,
provenance, file structure and limb dominance are all constant within a session.
All four were verified at chance (max |AUC − 0.5| = 0.051). That task clears its
pre-registered bar at **0.610**.

**The finding.** Real measured monocular error costs **0.027–0.033 AUC**, taking
0.610 to **0.583** against a chance rate of 0.517. The camera is not the
bottleneck — the signal is. Five independent probes confirm the ceiling is
physiological rather than a modelling artefact.

## 6. Why no product was built

A per-runner injury readout would misrepresent the evidence. At the realistic
operating point:

| measure | value |
|---|---|
| best precision (PPV) | **0.574** against a 0.517 base rate |
| sensitivity at 90% specificity | **0.130** |
| calibration slope | **0.716** (1.0 is perfect) |
| Brier improvement over a constant | **1.5%** |
| **agreement on repeat scans of the same runner** | **0.667** |

And averaging a runner's repeat sessions does **not** help (0.587 → 0.583). If
the error were random noise, averaging would reduce it. It doesn't — so the error
is **subject-specific**: the model is consistently wrong about the same people.
That closes the "just scan them twice" escape route.

## 7. Where to attack this

The honest soft spots, listed so review is efficient rather than a hunt.

1. **The dose-response test used a proxy.** `InjDefn` measures how much an injury
   disrupted *training*, not mechanical severity. A runner training through
   chronic pain may show more asymmetry than one who missed two workouts with a
   resolved strain. The flat result (ρ = +0.044, CI spanning zero) is evidence
   against a physiological signal but is **not a knockout alone** — it leans on
   agreement with the other two diagnostics.
2. **Repeatability rests on non-independent pairs.** 258 pairs from 72 subjects,
   so a subject with 8 sessions contributes 28. The 0.667 is an estimate with
   real uncertainty — though not plausibly near 1.0.
3. **The +0.54° detector cost is a proxy on a biased subsample.** 200 clips
   deliberately weighted toward long ones, not the full 592, and it bounds
   "generic COCO detector", not Keypoint R-CNN specifically.
4. **120 fps is inferred**, from stride cadence with an assumed 2.5–3.0 strides/s.
   The tool's rate-matching step depends on it.
5. **The video tool has never seen real footage.** Exercised on synthetic video
   and on AthleticsPose keypoints only.
6. **One dataset, one protocol.** Treadmill running, stance phase only, a single
   clinic. The negative result's *generality* rests entirely on that, and no
   external validation exists.
7. **Two documented protocol deviations.** The labelling rule departs from the
   dataset README (a recorded diagnosis outranks a blank severity field), and
   phase 1's kill criterion fired but work continued onto the within-subject
   task. Both are recorded rather than hidden, but a reviewer should weigh them.
8. **Waveforms come from a patched MATLAB pipeline.** It reproduces the archive's
   own `dv_r` exactly — which is the check that it is faithful — but it is both a
   reproducibility barrier and a possible error source.

## 8. Decisions needed

**1. Is the project complete?**
The research is exhausted — five independent probes say the ceiling is the
signal. The video tool is optional.
*Recommendation: yes for the research; treat the tool as a separate question.*

**2. Should phase 3's headline become 3.18°?**
The inference fixes are real and worth −0.25°. But 3.4° is what phase 3 actually
ran, and rewriting it would misrepresent the record.
*Recommendation: no — keep 3.4° with a pointer to phase 7. Reasonable people
could disagree, so it is a decision rather than something settled quietly.*

**3. Re-run the detector cost at full n?**
~40 minutes. Removes the subsample caveat from §7.3.
*Recommendation: yes — it is cheap and the number appears in the tool's output.*

**4. Close the gap between the research and the tool?**
The tool emits continuous traces; the research models consume 101-point
stance-normalised curves. Without stride segmentation there is **no path from a
video to the analysis that took seven phases to build**. 1–2 days plus
validation.
*Recommendation: only if the tool matters beyond demonstration.*

**5. External write-up?**
*Recommendation: worth serious consideration.* A null this well controlled is
unusual — pre-registered bars, negative controls re-asserted per script, five
independent ceiling probes, and a documented corrections ledger. The rigour is
the most valuable output here, more than the finding itself.
